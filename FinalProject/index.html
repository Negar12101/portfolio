Uneven Warming

Exploring temperature change across countries, 1961–2024

Negar Ashktorab · Individual project · Instructor approved

Project Overview

This project extends my Project 3 Temperature Change Explorer into an interactive explanation of temperature change across eight selected countries.

I will investigate how long-term patterns differ and whether countries experience similar year-to-year changes.

The dataset contains annual surface temperature anomalies relative to the 1951–1980 baseline, covering 1961–2024.

The analysis focuses on the United States, Canada, Brazil, the United Kingdom, Iran, India, China, and Australia.

Separate country panels with shared axes and trailing 10-year averages will distinguish sustained patterns from annual variability.

A period heatmap and a comparison of two 20-year means will show how the selected countries differ across time.

A correlation matrix will compare annual anomaly levels with year-to-year changes over a user-selected period.

A playable timeline will show annual country rankings using a fixed temperature scale.

The final D3 website will develop these views into a guided explanation with linked time filters, country selection, and annotations for a general audience.

The goal is to explain differences between long-term change, annual variability, and statistical association without presenting the selected countries as a global estimate.

A temperature anomaly is a difference from a historical baseline, not an absolute temperature. The eight countries illustrate different geographic settings but are not a representative sample of the world. Their unweighted mean must not be interpreted as a global temperature estimate.

Data validation requires complete annual observations for the selected countries. Missing values are not replaced with zero. Period comparisons use 1961–1980 and 2005–2024, with 20 observations in each period. Trailing 10-year averages require a complete window and begin in 1970.

Pearson correlation describes linear association, not causation or equal warming rates. Correlations of annual anomaly levels may reflect shared time trends. Comparing year-to-year changes provides a complementary view of annual variation, but does not remove every source of dependence. No statistical significance claims are made.
