About This Prototype

This project extends my Project 3 Temperature Change Explorer into an interactive explanation of temperature change across eight selected countries.

The central question is how long-term patterns differ and whether countries experience similar year-to-year changes. The data cover 1961–2024 and measure temperature anomalies relative to the 1951–1980 baseline.

Begin with the annual summary, then compare individual countries and period averages. In the correlation matrix, switch between annual anomalies and year-to-year changes to explore two different forms of association.

Finally, play the timeline or select a year to compare annual country rankings. This is an initial prototype; the final version will develop the narrative and connections between these views.

Project 3: Temperature Change Explorer

A temperature anomaly is a difference from a historical baseline, not an absolute temperature. These eight countries illustrate different geographic settings but are not a representative sample of the world. Their unweighted mean is not a global temperature estimate.

Data validation requires complete observations for the selected countries. Missing values are not replaced with zero. Period comparisons use 1961–1980 and 2005–2024, with 20 observations in each period. Trailing 10-year averages require a complete window and begin in 1970.

Pearson correlation describes linear association, not causation or equal warming rates. Correlations of annual anomaly levels may reflect shared time trends. Comparing year-to-year changes provides a complementary view of annual variation, but does not remove every source of dependence. No statistical significance claims are made.

What Have I Done So Far?

I have developed a D3 prototype that loads and validates temperature anomaly observations for eight selected countries from 1961 to 2024. The page includes six views covering an annual selected-country mean, individual country trajectories, period averages, correlations, and annual rankings. I replaced overlapping country lines with separate panels that share identical axes, making differences easier to compare. I implemented a correlation matrix with time-period controls and a choice between annual anomalies and year-to-year changes. I also built a playable timeline with pause, reset, and year selection controls, while keeping the temperature scale fixed. The prototype is published through GitHub Pages and includes explanatory text about the baseline, sample selection, and interpretation of the statistics.

What Will Be the Most Challenging Part to Design, and Why?

The most challenging part will be turning the six exploratory views into a clear, connected explanation rather than a collection of separate charts. Readers need to understand the distinction between long-term warming, annual variability, and correlation, because these concepts answer different questions. Correlations of annual anomaly levels can reflect shared time trends, so the interface must explain why switching to year-to-year changes can produce different results. I plan to add a more guided narrative, targeted annotations, and clearer connections between time selections across views. I will also refine the animated comparison so that changing rankings remain readable and do not distract from the magnitude of the anomalies. Finally, I need to balance these explanations with responsive layouts and accessible controls so that the project remains usable on smaller screens and without relying on animation.

An Emerging Takeaway

A shared long-term warming pattern does not mean that countries experience identical annual changes. Comparing annual anomaly levels with year-to-year differences helps separate these two questions. The final project will develop this distinction into a guided explanation.
