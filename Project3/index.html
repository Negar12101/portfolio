How do annual temperature anomalies differ across locations and time periods?

Question and audience

This visualization explores how annual temperature anomalies differ across locations and time periods. It is intended for readers who want to compare climate records without working directly with a spreadsheet. Users can select two locations and a year range to examine similarities, differences, and year-to-year variation.

The exploratory charts examine the World record, country comparisons, period averages, the distribution of recent anomalies, yearly patterns, and missing observations. The interactive design focuses on comparing two records at a time so that the display remains readable.

Design decisions

Lines and shared axes

Horizontal position represents year, and vertical position represents temperature anomaly in degrees Celsius. Lines connect consecutive available annual observations to show how each record changes over time. Both locations share the same axes, supporting direct comparison.

The vertical scale remains fixed across location and year selections. This prevents changes in scale from making one selection appear more variable than another. The tradeoff is extra empty space for locations with a narrower range of values.

Color and line style

Purple identifies the first selection and orange identifies the second. A solid line and a dashed line provide an additional distinction, so color is not the only way to identify the records. The legend and control labels use the same colors as their corresponding lines.

Interactive exploration

Two location menus let readers choose their own comparison. The year controls allow them to examine shorter periods within the full record. Hovering over a point reveals its location, year, and exact value. The Reset years button restores the complete time range.

These controls support different questions without displaying every location at once. For example, readers can compare two countries over the full record, then narrow the period to inspect recent annual variation. Selecting the same location twice displays a message explaining that the lines overlap.

Alternatives and tradeoffs

Plotting every location at once would create substantial overlap. A map could show geographic patterns for a particular year, but would make detailed comparison over time more difficult. The two-line design prioritizes temporal comparison while keeping the interface focused.

Dropdown menus provide precise selections, although they require more steps than directly brushing a time interval. Showing individual annual observations preserves short-term variation, but it also produces a less smooth display than a moving average.

Data and transformations

The supplied IMF Climate Change Indicators dataset contains annual temperature anomalies from 1961 through 2024, using FAOSTAT data. Values are measured relative to the 1951–1980 baseline. A value of zero means the baseline average, not an absolute temperature of zero degrees Celsius.

The original CSV stores each year in a separate column. The JavaScript converts these columns into annual records for each location and parses available values as numbers. Empty or invalid values remain missing. They are not replaced with zero, and the lines break at missing years. No smoothing or filling of missing observations is applied.

Selecting a year range filters the observations displayed in the chart. Availability counts report how many annual observations exist within that range. The menu includes countries, territories, and supplied regional aggregates; these geographic units should be distinguished when interpreting a comparison.

Interpretation and limitations

These values describe differences from each location's baseline climate. They do not show which location has the higher absolute temperature. Short periods may emphasize temporary fluctuations, so readers should also examine the complete record. The visualization describes patterns and does not establish their causes.

Data coverage differs across locations and years. Missing observations can limit comparisons. Regional aggregates are taken directly from the supplied dataset; this visualization does not calculate them by averaging the country records.

Development process

I worked individually on this project, using AI assistance to help develop and revise the code. I explored the dataset, implemented the suggested changes, and checked the results in the browser. I adjusted the colors and tested the location selectors, year filters, tooltips, and Reset button.

I spent approximately four hours on the project. About three hours were spent understanding and preparing the data. The remaining hour was spent on implementation, styling, and checking the visualization.

Data preparation was the most challenging part because the original CSV stored each year in a separate column. The code converts these columns into annual records for each location. Missing values are preserved as gaps rather than converted to zero, which avoids displaying observations that do not exist.
